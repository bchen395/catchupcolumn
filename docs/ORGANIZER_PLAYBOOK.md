# The Organizer's Playbook

**Written 2026-09-16.** One page for the person starting a Group.

The whole distribution model is *one organizer brings six friends*. That job has
only ever been done by the person who built the app, which means it has never
really been tested — he knows why every screen looks the way it does, he will
nag people he's known for a decade, and he will absorb friction a normal
organizer would quit over. This page exists so the job can be handed to someone
else and watched.

If you are that someone else: everything below is a draft, not a script. Change
the words to sound like you. **Note what you changed** — that's the part worth
reporting back.

---

## The one job

Get six or seven people to write a few paragraphs each, one week at a time,
until it stops feeling like a favor to you and starts feeling like a thing you
all do.

Nothing else matters in the first month. Not the photos, not the Group name, not
whether everyone has the app.

---

## Before you start — 15 minutes

1. **Write down the names.** Six to eight people, by name, on paper. Not "my
   college friends" — the actual list. If you can't get to six, the Group is
   the wrong shape; pick a tighter circle rather than padding it out.
2. **Name the Group.** It goes at the top of the paper every week. "The Sunday
   Dispatch," "Cohort 11 Weekly," "The Williams Family Weekly." Not clever —
   theirs.
3. **Pick the day it arrives.** Monday morning is the default and it is the
   right one: people write over the weekend, the paper lands when the week
   starts. Set the publish time for the zone most of them live in.

---

## Step 1 — The ask

**One person at a time. Never a group text.** A group text makes it an
announcement that everyone can ignore in front of everyone else. A 1:1 text
makes it a thing a friend asked you to do.

Send something close to this:

> Random, but — I miss you and I hate that catching up now means seeing that you
> exist on Instagram. I'm starting a thing: seven of us write a few paragraphs
> about how our week actually went, and Monday morning it arrives as one small
> newspaper. No feed, no notifications, nothing to scroll. Twenty minutes on a
> Sunday and then it leaves you alone. Want in?

When they ask what it is:

> An app called Catch Up Column, but you mostly don't touch it — it emails you
> everyone's week. That's the whole thing.

Three rules for this step:

- **Don't pitch the app.** Nobody wants another app. They want the Monday email
  and the reason to write. The app is where you type; say that and move on.
- **Don't oversell the concept.** "Digital minimalism," "the attention economy,"
  "a ritual" — skip all of it. Most of your people have never thought about any
  of that and don't need to.
- **Ask for a yes, not a maybe.** "Want in?" beats "would you maybe be
  interested in something like this?" — the second one gets a polite yes from
  people who will never write.

*Time: ~5 minutes per person. ~35 minutes total. Do it in one sitting.*

## Step 2 — Write the first one yourself, before anyone else has to

Send your own entry in first, the day you finish the asks. Make it ordinary and
a little unflattering — a bad week at work, the thing you're avoiding, what you
ate. You are setting the register, and the register is *letter to a friend*, not
*update from a brand*.

If your first entry is a highlight reel, everyone else writes a highlight reel,
and the Group is dead in three weeks because nobody has anything impressive to
report.

*Time: 20 minutes.*

## Step 3 — The Saturday nag

Two days before it publishes, text the people who haven't written. Individually.

> paper goes out Monday — you in this week? doesn't have to be good, three
> sentences is a real entry

**This is the job.** For the first month you are the reason it happens. Expect
to do it every week and don't read it as failure: the ritual is not yet a
ritual, and you are the scaffolding until it is.

What not to do: don't apologize for nagging, don't make it a guilt trip, and
don't post a running tally of who has and hasn't written. The moment this feels
like a chore with a scoreboard, it becomes the thing they left.

*Time: ~10 minutes.*

## Step 4 — Monday, when it lands

Read it. Reply to one person about one specific thing they wrote — by text, not
in the app. There is nowhere in the product to leave a comment and there never
will be, so the conversation the paper starts happens off it. That is working as
intended, and you modeling it once is what teaches everyone else to do it.

*Time: 10 minutes.*

## Step 5 — Weeks 2 through 4

Repeat steps 3 and 4. Two things to watch for:

- **The thin edition.** Some week, two people write. Send it anyway and say
  nothing about it being thin. A short paper is a letter; an apology for a short
  paper is an announcement that the thing is failing.
- **The first unprompted entry.** Someone writes without you asking. That is the
  only real signal in the first month — note the week it happens, or that it
  didn't.

*Time: ~30 minutes per week, steady state.*

## When it goes wrong

**"I'm not going to download an app."** Fine — don't fight it. Take their entry
by text and put it in for them. During Group Zero specifically, message the
owner and he'll set the account up so the byline is theirs, not yours; see
[POSITIONING.md](./POSITIONING.md) §6, "Posting for someone who hasn't
installed." Never paste someone's writing under your own name.

**Nobody writes in week 1.** Normal. Nag, and check that the ask landed as
personal rather than as a broadcast. If week 2 is also empty, it's worth asking
one person directly what stopped them — the answer is almost always "I forgot"
or "I didn't know what to say," and those need opposite fixes.

**Someone says it's another app to fix apps.** The honest answer is the whole
positioning: *it wants twenty minutes a week and then it goes away.* Don't argue
past that.

**You're doing all the writing.** Stop for one week. Don't write your own entry,
don't nag, see what arrives. Painful, and the fastest way to find out whether
there is a Group here or just you.

---

## If you're running Group B — what to report back

You are the experiment, so the interesting data is where this page was wrong:

- Which steps did you skip, and why?
- Which words did you change because they didn't sound like you?
- Where did you need to know something this page doesn't say?
- How many of your six said yes? How many wrote in week 1? In week 4?
- At any point, did you consider quitting? What was happening that week?

The one thing this page cannot fake is whether a person who didn't build the app
will do the recruiting work at all. If the answer turns out to be no, that is
the most valuable finding in the whole project, and it is worth knowing in
October rather than next year.
